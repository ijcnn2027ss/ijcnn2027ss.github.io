SPECIAL SESSION PROPOSAL

Knowledge-Enhanced AI for Real-World Applications

SESSION ABSTRACT

Neural networks increasingly support decisions in settings where relevant knowledge is scattered across documents, images, sensor streams, and domain rules. In education, a tutoring system may need to connect a learner’s responses to curriculum concepts. In transportation, a perception system may need to combine sensor evidence with maps or traffic rules under changing conditions. These settings motivate a shared research question: how can neural models use and update domain knowledge while staying reliable when evidence is incomplete, noisy, or changing?

This special session will bring together researchers who build neural models and those who evaluate them in real applications. It will examine how different forms of knowledge enter a model, through retrieval, multimodal grounding, learned representations, or explicit constraints, and how their contribution can be measured. This cross-domain focus connects IJCNN research on neural representation learning, reasoning, and adaptation with practical questions about robustness, evidence quality, and the cost of deployment.

Topics of Interest Include

  • Knowledge representation and reasoning, including knowledge graphs, graph neural networks, domain priors, and neuro-symbolic methods.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation, evidence-grounded question answering, multimodal foundation models, and tool-using AI agents.
  • Recommender systems, user and learner modeling, knowledge tracing, and adaptive learning systems.
  • Knowledge-enhanced visual recognition, multimedia retrieval, sensor fusion, and applications in intelligent transportation, remote sensing, healthcare, and scientific discovery.
  • Continual knowledge updating, explainability, and benchmarks or system evaluations that measure evidence use, robustness, or deployment efficiency.

The session welcomes original methods, application studies, and benchmark or system papers that demonstrate how knowledge or contextual evidence improves a neural system under realistic conditions.

PROSPECTIVE INVITED PAPERS

The entries below are proposed invitations. Titles, author lists, and abstracts are tentative and subject to confirmation by the invited teams.

1. Graph-Grounded Evidence Fusion for Video Forgery Detection in Compressed Footage

Yue Zhao (Shandong Police College) and Ruimin Hu (Wuhan University)

Compression can obscure individual traces of video manipulation. This study would represent facial motion, temporal inconsistencies, and compression-sensitive cues as a graph of forensic observations. Graph reasoning would combine weak clues across frames and identify the clips supporting a prediction. Cross-dataset and recompression tests would measure detection accuracy and the reliability of evidence localization.

2. Curriculum-Grounded Multimodal Classification of Visual Assessment Questions

Jing Wang (School of Automation, Chongqing University of Posts and Telecommunications)

Classifying diagram-based assessment questions requires recovering concepts that may be implicit in both the prompt and the visual layout. This study would align question text and diagram regions with descriptions of curriculum concepts. The evaluation would test concept classification under sparse labels and transfer across grade levels or subject areas.

3. User-Guided Evidence Correction in Multimodal Question Answering

Yue Gu (School of Information Management, Wuhan University) and Zheng Wang (School of Computer Science, Wuhan University)

This study would let users inspect the image regions and retrieved passages behind an AI-generated answer, correct misleading evidence, and request a revised answer. The system would use those corrections to update its evidence selection. A controlled user study would compare task accuracy, correction effort, and trust calibration against a standard answer interface and a read-only evidence interface.

4. Failure-Guided Data Synthesis for Efficient Vision-Language Model Adaptation

Xin Zhang and Joey Tianyi Zhou (A*STAR Centre for Frontier AI Research, Singapore)

This study would use diagnostic errors from a vision-language model to generate targeted examples for difficult concepts and rare visual conditions. A data selection stage would keep a compact synthetic set for adaptation. Under matched data and training budgets, the evaluation would compare targeted synthesis with generic augmentation and dataset distillation on rare cases and under domain shift.

5. Attribute-Guided Few-Shot Crop Cultivar Recognition Under Field Shift

Xiaohan Yu (School of Computing, Macquarie University)

Closely related cultivars can differ in subtle morphological traits whose visibility changes with growth stage and imaging conditions. This study would align image regions with expert descriptors of diagnostic traits and use the alignment to adapt to cultivars with few labeled examples. Evaluation across sites and growth stages would measure classification accuracy and agreement with expert-identified visual evidence.
