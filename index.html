SPECIAL SESSION PROPOSAL

Knowledge-Enhanced AI for Real-World Applications

SPECIAL SESSION ABSTRACT

Neural networks increasingly support decisions in settings where relevant knowledge is scattered across documents, images, sensor streams, and domain rules. In education, a tutoring system may need to connect a learner’s responses to curriculum concepts. In transportation, a perception system may need to combine sensor evidence with maps or traffic rules under changing conditions. These settings motivate a shared research question: how can neural models use and update domain knowledge while staying reliable when evidence is incomplete, noisy, or changing?

This special session will bring together researchers who build neural models and those who evaluate them in real applications. It will examine how different forms of knowledge enter a model, through retrieval, multimodal grounding, learned representations, or explicit constraints, and how their contribution can be measured. This cross-domain focus connects IJCNN research on neural representation learning, reasoning, and adaptation with practical questions about robustness, evidence quality, and the cost of deployment.

Topics of Interest Include

  • Knowledge representation and reasoning, including knowledge graphs, graph neural networks, domain priors, and neuro-symbolic methods.
  • Retrieval-augmented generation, evidence-grounded question answering, multimodal foundation models, and tool-using AI agents.
  • Recommender systems, user and learner modeling, knowledge tracing, and adaptive learning systems.
  • Knowledge-enhanced visual recognition, multimedia retrieval, sensor fusion, and applications in intelligent transportation, remote sensing, healthcare, and scientific discovery.
  • Continual knowledge updating, explainability, and benchmarks or system evaluations that measure evidence use, robustness, or deployment efficiency.

The session welcomes original methods, application studies, and benchmark or system papers that demonstrate how knowledge or contextual evidence improves a neural system under realistic conditions.

KEY SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS

⭐ Double-Blind Review

IJCNN 2027 follows a double-blind review process, in which reviewers and authors remain anonymous to each other. Authors must ensure that their initial submissions do not reveal their identities. Authors' names must not be included in the submitted PDF. Please use "Anonymous Authors" in the author field of the IEEE template, and refer to previous work in the third person wherever possible. Preprints are allowed and do not violate the double-blind policy. Publicly available information, such as online technical reports, that may allow reviewers to infer authors' identities does not itself constitute a violation of the policy.

Important: Anonymization is mandatory. Papers that explicitly or implicitly reveal authors' identities may be rejected. For the camera-ready version, authors' names and affiliations must be included in the IEEE template format.

⭐ Manuscript Format

All submissions must be in PDF format and use US Letter paper size. Manuscripts must follow a double-column, single-spaced layout, using 10-point Times Roman font. Papers should be 4–6 pages, including figures, tables, references, and appendices. Do not include page numbers; the conference organizers will insert them. Authors must use the official IEEE conference proceedings templates. Violations of these formatting requirements may result in rejection.

⭐ Page Limit and Extra Page Fees

Standard submissions include up to 6 pages at no additional page charge. Up to 4 additional pages are permitted, allowing a maximum of 10 pages, at a charge of US$100 per extra page.

Extra page charges are calculated based on the maximum number of pages exceeding six across the initial and final submissions. Papers initially submitted with more than 6 pages remain subject to extra page charges, even if the final camera-ready version is reduced to 6 pages or fewer. These charges will be billed together with the registration fees.

⭐ Originality

All submissions must be original and must not be currently under review at another venue. Papers will be checked for plagiarism. The IJCNN 2027 Program Committee reserves the right to desk-reject any paper containing material suspected of being plagiarized.

⭐ AI-Generated Text Disclosure

The use of artificial intelligence (AI)-generated text in a submitted paper must be disclosed in the acknowledgements section. The sections of the paper containing AI-generated text must also include a citation to the AI system used to generate that text, in accordance with IEEE publication requirements.

IMPORTANT REMINDER: For special session papers, please select the corresponding Special Session title from the Research Topics list in the submission system to ensure that your paper is properly routed for review.

For detailed submission instructions, formatting requirements and other policies, please refer to the official IJCNN 2027 author guidelines.
